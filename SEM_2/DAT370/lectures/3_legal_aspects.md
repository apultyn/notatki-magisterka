# Legal aspects of pentesting - 9.09.2026
## Intro
- legal $\centernot \implies$ ethical
- ethical $\centernot \implies$ legal
- if you are told by government employer to break the law, you still might be liable
- not every country comply to the international law (well well well)
- there is no "world" police, so international law is in force only if states are able to enforce it
- UN Charter Article 2 - all members shall refrain from the threat or use of force of political independence of any State
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights Article 12 - people's privacy, family, home and correspondence should not be interfered with
- in theory not relevant conventions, but in fact e.g Geneva Conventions prohibits hackers from attacking civilian infrastructure
	- you can be treated as a threat to humanity and even executed
## UN Convention against Cybercrime
- not yet ratified and thus not in power
- open until the end of 2026
- tries to address illegal access, illegal interception, data and system interference, misuse of devices, forgery, theft and fraud, child sexual abuse, money laundering e.t.c
## International standards
- ISO 27001
- PCI-DSS
- mostly applies to Infosec (security of data)
- you need to know how they require you to do your job
## EU Framework
- loads of acts: GDPR/DGA, sectorial regulations (e.g AI Act), DGA/DSA, Cyber Solidarity Act, Chat Control, eIDAS 
- different law methods:
	- law is passed nationally and must be followed
	- EU regulation - EU-wide rule, becomes law in all members automatically
	- EU directive - EU sets goal, countries decide how to reach it
### GDPR (RODO)
- in Poland it's RODO
- protecting personal data
- rights including access, rectification and deletion
- may affect your work if you work with real data
### DGA
- defines rules of data handling in the cloud
- data transfer
### Sectorial regulations
- relevant for specific sectors (e.g high-risk AI)
- similar to standards
### DSA
- online platforms and marketplaces
- enforces companies to actively mitigate cyberthreats, illegal content and frauds
### Cyber Solidarity Act
- established interconnected national cyberhubs to detect threats and share intelligence
- cybersecurity testing in critical sectors
- mutual assistance with attacks
- for example when Spain was cyber-attacked by Morocco, other EU countries sent help
### Chat Control
- implements voluntary filters to prevent Child Sexual Abuse Material (CSAM)
- big privacy discussion
- clashes with end-to-end encryption, since filters must apply on decrypted data
### eIDAS
- regulates secure identity and signature services
- e.g BankID or EduSign
### Reverse engineering
- EU allows it under certain circumstances 
- you can't do it e.g for money purposes
- allowed for creating interoperability of software
## Swedish Law
- as in Poland, they have Civil Law and Penal Law
### Civil Law
- compensation law
- compensating damages you cause
- especially if intentional or careless
- only if the other person did make an effort to mitigate losses
### Brottsbalken (Penal Law)
- Computer intrusion - accessing data without permission, changing, destroying, blocking or adding data - fines and jail
- Stalking - limited in what constitutes a crime but still fairly wide
- Slander - pointing out someone as criminal with some exceptions (forced to share information by police, circumstances play a role, if true or reasonable to be true)
- Fraud - tricking someone into doing something that benefits the perpetrator and harms the victim, it applies also to giving false information to an automated process and not a person
- Unauthorised use of identity - also impersonating someone, but for this law to apply, damaging the impersonated person is required
