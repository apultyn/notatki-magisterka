# Notes generation prompt

I will upload lecture slides (PDFs) and potentially additional materials such as textbook chapters, scientific papers, or publications.

Your task is to generate a **comprehensive, well-structured PDF study guide** based on these materials, suitable for preparing for a university examination.

## 1. Content requirements

- Cover **all important topics, concepts, algorithms, mechanisms, and architectural principles** from the provided materials.
- Explain concepts thoroughly, rather than simply copying or shortening slide bullet points.
- Make the notes **completely self-contained**. I should be able to learn and understand everything important without opening the original slides.
- Expand brief slide statements into meaningful explanations, including how mechanisms work, why they are used, and their advantages, disadvantages, and limitations.
- Include relevant definitions, examples, comparisons, and step-by-step explanations of complex processes.
- For algorithms, describe their purpose, assumptions, operation, complexity (where relevant), and practical applications.
- For technical concepts, explain the reasoning behind them, not merely their definitions.
- Pay particular attention to information that could appear in theoretical or practical examination questions.
- Avoid unnecessary repetition, filler, and excessively simplified explanations.
- Preserve the terminology and technical accuracy of the original materials. If additional background knowledge is needed for clarity, distinguish it from the source material.

## 2. Structure and organization

- Organize the notes into logically numbered sections and subsections, following the lecture's progression.
- **Every main numbered section must start on a new PDF page.**
- Keep related concepts together and use clear subsection headings.
- Use tables to compare approaches, architectures, algorithms, or technologies when appropriate.
- Include diagrams or simple illustrations when they significantly help explain a concept.
- Use mathematical notation and pseudocode where necessary.
- Highlight important definitions, distinctions, and examination takeaways.
- Include a concise summary of the most important concepts.
- Finish with **15–25 exam-style questions and model answers** covering the material.

## 3. References to slides

- Do not write phrases such as "as shown on slide X", "refer to the diagram", or "the slides explain".
- Instead, include the relevant information directly in the notes, explaining diagrams in words or reproducing simplified versions where useful.
- References to slide numbers or source ranges may appear in **main section headings only**.
- Never assume I can consult the original slides while reading.

## 4. Additional publications and reading materials

If I provide scientific papers, articles, or textbook chapters:

- Give each major publication its **own dedicated section**, clearly separated from the lecture notes.
- Explain the publication's background, motivation, main arguments, methodology (where relevant), evidence, findings, and conclusions.
- Include important statistics, case studies, technical observations, and real-world examples.
- Explain the implications of the findings and their relationship to the lecture topics.
- Discuss limitations or qualifications of the publication's conclusions.
- Make this section detailed enough that I can answer examination questions about the publication without reading the original.

## 5. PDF formatting

Create a professional, clean, readable academic PDF.

- A4 portrait format.
- Consistent headings, fonts, spacing, and margins.
- Numbered pages.
- Clickable table of contents and PDF bookmarks.
- Every main section starts on a new page.
- Avoid large empty areas or unnecessary blank pages.
- Ensure tables, figures, code, and mathematical expressions fit properly.
- Avoid splitting important tables or diagrams across pages.
- Use restrained colors for visual hierarchy.

## 6. Quality requirements

Before delivering the PDF:

1. Check that all major topics from the provided materials are covered.
2. Verify that every explanation is understandable independently of the original slides.
3. Check that the additional publications are covered in sufficient depth.
4. Ensure there are no missing explanations, unfinished sentences, or placeholder text.
5. Verify page breaks, PDF rendering, diagrams, and formatting.
6. Ensure the document prioritizes **completeness and clarity over brevity**.

Do not impose an arbitrary page limit. The notes should be as long as necessary to cover the material properly without becoming unnecessarily verbose.

**Deliver the final result as a downloadable PDF file.**
